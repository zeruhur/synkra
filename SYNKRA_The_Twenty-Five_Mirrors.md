# SYNKRA: The Twenty-Five Mirrors

## Premise

**SYNKRA: The Twenty-Five Mirrors** is a contemporary system of symbolic reflection, inspired by the analytical psychology of Carl Gustav Jung. It was created in 2025. It claims no ancient origins and does not attribute its symbols to any historical or cultural tradition.

The system is not a tool for prediction and it is not a form of therapy. It is an aid to self-reflection.

### Foundation

The mechanism the system relies on is **projection**: faced with an open symbol, a person recognizes in it contents that belong to them and that would otherwise stay implicit. The drawn glyph "knows" nothing about the situation: it is the consultant's reading that gives it meaning.

Jung's concept of **synchronicity** (the principle of acausal connection between a psychic event and an external event) is the frame that inspired the system. Consultants are free to adopt it as a reading attitude, but SYNKRA does not presuppose it and does not claim any real link between the draw and the consultant's fate. The system works on a purely projective basis as well.

## Architecture

SYNKRA is a **5×5 matrix**. Each glyph sits at the crossing of two axes:

- a **dimension** (the row): the area of experience in which something happens;
- a **phase** (the column): the point in the process where that something stands.

The meaning of each glyph can therefore be read in two ways: from its name and concept, or from its position in the matrix. Anyone who knows the five dimensions and the five phases can interpret a glyph without remembering its definition.

### The five dimensions

| Code | Dimension | Scope | Base shape |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| T | TRANSFORMATION | The changing self, endings and beginnings, cycles | Circle |
| R | RELATION | The space shared between self and other | Mandorla (intersection of two circles) |
| M | MOVEMENT | Action, direction, drive | Triangle, apex up |
| K | KNOWLEDGE | The gaze on what is not yet known | Rhombus |
| I | INTEGRATION | Structure, the relation between parts and whole | Square |

### The five phases

| Phase | Name | Sense | Mark on the base shape |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 1 | Latency | What is present but not yet active | A dot at the center |
| 2 | Crisis | What breaks or opens | A gap in the outline, at the top |
| 3 | Process | What is under way and cannot be seen from outside | A horizontal wavy line inside |
| 4 | Manifestation | What comes out and becomes visible | A vertical line from the center that crosses the outline at the top |
| 5 | Completion | What closes and integrates | A second, smaller, concentric copy of the shape |

### Visual grammar

Each glyph is the base shape of its dimension with the mark of its phase. All lines are thin and black, on a white ground. There are no other elements.

This rule has three consequences:

- two different glyphs cannot be confused, because they always differ in base shape, in phase mark, or in both;
- anyone who learns 10 elements (5 shapes and 5 marks) can read all 25 glyphs;
- glyphs of the same phase read as "relatives" across different dimensions.

With such a small geometric vocabulary, some shapes resemble existing symbols (for example the circle with a central dot, or the mandorla). Within the system, the glyphs carry only the meaning given in this document.

### Names and codes

Each glyph has an invented **proper name**, built on recognizable Latin, Greek and Germanic roots to make it easier to remember (for example GERMA from Latin *germen*, sprout, or XUNDA from Latin *exundare*, to overflow).

Each name begins with a different letter. The 25 initials use every letter of the alphabet except Y. This means each glyph can be referred to by a single letter.

Each glyph also has a **code** that gives its position in the matrix (for example T1 for GERMA). The initial identifies the glyph; the code shows its dimension and phase at a glance.

## Materials

SYNKRA can use any medium that allows a random draw without replacement: 25 identical tiles in a bag, 25 cards, or two five-sided dice (or a d10 halved) for row and column, rerolling if the glyph has already come up.

Glyphs have no upright or reversed orientation. The light and shadow of a glyph are read from context and position, not from orientation.

## Methods of consultation

### Reading procedure

1. Frame the question openly (for example "What am I overlooking in this situation?"), not as a yes/no question.
2. Draw the glyphs and place them in the positions of the chosen method.
3. Look at each shape before checking its meaning, and note your first spontaneous reaction.
4. Read the meaning and the shadow of each glyph and weigh which pole is closer to the situation.
5. Read the matrix of the spread (see below).
6. Read the relations between the glyphs in their positions.

### Reading the matrix

In spreads of three or more glyphs, the matrix offers a second layer of reading:

- **Repeated dimension**: the situation plays out mainly in that area.
- **Repeated phase**: the situation stands mainly at that point in the process (for example, several phase 2 glyphs point to a crisis spread across several levels).
- **Missing dimension or phase**: an area or a moment that may be neglected.
- **Distant phases**: phase 1 and phase 5 glyphs in the same spread suggest that something is ending while something else has not yet begun.

### Single draw

One glyph. Immediate reflection on a specific theme.

### Triad

Three glyphs in a row, from left to right. Two variants:

- **Time**: past, present, tendency
- **Knot**: situation, obstacle, resource

### Quincunx

Five glyphs arranged like the five pips on a die. It maps the tensions active in a complex situation.

| Position | Sense |
| :---- | :---- |
| Center | The core of the matter |
| Top | What is conscious |
| Bottom | What remains in shadow |
| Left | What holds back |
| Right | What pushes forward |

### Personal methods

Users may create other methods. Every position must have a defined sense before the draw.

### Short notation

A reading can be recorded in two ways. With initials, in the order center, top, bottom, left, right: `M E H P C` (MIREN, EMRAL, HULMEN, PARTEN, CYRKEL). With codes, when dimensions and phases should be visible at a glance: `R3 T4 K1 I2 M5`.

## Limits

SYNKRA is a tool for self-reflection. It does not replace the support of a psychologist or other professional. If a reading brings up persistent distress, the right step is to talk about it with a qualified person.
